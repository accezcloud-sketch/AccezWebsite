## The Regulatory Landscape of Rent Increases and Lease Renewals

The Saudi Arabian rental market operates under a regulatory framework designed to protect both tenants and landlords, and it changed significantly in September 2025. Under measures approved by the Council of Ministers and enacted by royal decree, the Real Estate General Authority introduced a **five-year freeze on rent increases in Riyadh**, [as reported by Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2616697/amp):

- From 25 September 2025, landlords within Riyadh's urban boundaries cannot increase rent on **residential or commercial** properties, in new or existing contracts.
- A vacant unit that was leased before must be re-let at the value of its last registered contract; for a unit never leased before, the rent is agreed between landlord and tenant.
- In Riyadh, a landlord cannot refuse a renewal the tenant wants, except for non-payment of rent, structural safety problems confirmed by an official technical report, or the personal need of the landlord or an immediate family member.
- Violations can bring fines of up to 12 months' rent for the unit, plus compensation for the affected party.
- The authority may extend the freeze to other cities or regions, with the approval of the Council of Economic and Development Affairs.

Outside Riyadh the freeze does not currently apply, so rent at renewal is a matter of agreement between the parties and of the registered contract. Across the Kingdom, however, **leases now renew automatically unless one party gives at least 60 days' notice before expiry**, and all lease agreements must be registered on Ejar. In practice this means any proposed change to the rent or terms has to be raised well before that 60-day window, documented, and reflected in the registered contract. Disputes are handled through official channels, which is why meticulous documentation matters; our guide to [handling tenant disputes and evictions the right way in Saudi Arabia](/blog/handling-tenant-disputes-and-evictions-the-right-way-in-saudi-arabia/) covers that process. The **Saudi Vision 2030** programme also places emphasis on quality of life, which includes stable and affordable housing.

## Best Practices for Property Managers

Adhering to the legal framework is only one part of effective property management. High-performing property managers also adopt proactive strategies to foster positive tenant relationships and ensure seamless lease renewals.

### Building Trust Through Clear Communication

One of the most effective strategies for property managers is to prioritize **transparent and consistent communication with tenants**. This means clearly outlining lease terms from the outset, including any clauses related to rent reviews or renewal processes. When it comes time for a lease renewal, especially if a rent increase is being proposed, communicating this well in advance and explaining the rationale behind it can significantly mitigate tenant concerns. Utilizing digital platforms for communication, a practice increasingly adopted across Saudi Arabia, can streamline this process, ensuring messages are delivered and acknowledged.

### Proactive Lease Renewal Management

Instead of waiting until the last minute, proactive managers begin the lease renewal conversation with tenants several months before the current lease expires, and always before the 60-day notice window opens. This approach allows ample time for negotiation, addressing any tenant concerns, and finalizing new agreements without undue pressure. It also gives managers a clear picture of their occupancy rates and potential revenue streams, which is vital for financial planning. By demonstrating foresight and a willingness to discuss terms collaboratively, property managers can often secure renewals at mutually agreeable rates, thereby reducing vacancy periods and the associated costs of finding new tenants.

## Navigating the Saudi Real Estate Ecosystem

Saudi Arabia's property sector is undergoing significant transformation, with technological adoption and regulatory alignment playing key roles. Property managers must be attuned to these shifts to remain competitive and compliant.

The Kingdom's ambitious **Vision 2030** reform plan heavily influences the real estate sector, aiming to increase homeownership rates and improve the overall living experience. This includes fostering a more transparent and efficient rental market. Central to this is the **Ejar platform**, the government system on which all rental contracts must be registered; our article on [Ejar compliance for property managers](/blog/ejar-compliance-made-simple-automating-saudi-rental-regulations/) explains the registration steps. Ejar provides a standardized contract framework, which enhances trust and reduces friction in the rental process. Furthermore, the widespread adoption of digital payment methods, such as **Mada and SADAD**, facilitated by entities like the Saudi Central Bank, has revolutionized how rent is collected, making it more convenient and secure for both parties. This digital shift aligns with the broader national objective of creating a thriving digital economy. The unique demand patterns around religious events like Hajj and Umrah also present specific considerations for property operators in certain regions, requiring flexible strategies for short-term rentals and lease management.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is there a cap on rent increases in Saudi Arabia?
"In Riyadh, rent increases on residential and commercial properties are frozen for five years from 25 September 2025, for new and existing contracts. The freeze does not currently apply to other cities, but the Real Estate General Authority can extend it with government approval, so check the current rules for your city."

### How much notice is needed to end or change a lease at renewal in Saudi Arabia?
"Leases renew automatically unless one party gives at least 60 days' notice before the contract expires. Any change to the terms should therefore be raised and agreed well before that point. In Riyadh, rent cannot be increased during the freeze."

### Is Ejar mandatory for all rental agreements in Saudi Arabia?
"Yes. All lease agreements, residential and commercial, must be registered on the Ejar platform. Either party can submit the contract, and the other party has 60 days to object before it is considered valid."

### Can I increase rent mid-lease in Saudi Arabia?
"The rent is set in the registered contract, so it cannot be raised unilaterally during the term. Any change needs a clause in the contract or the agreement of both parties, and in Riyadh increases are not permitted at all during the five-year freeze."
